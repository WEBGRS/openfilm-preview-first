# Preview first

## The result

Within a few minutes the person watches the whole film in Studio as a rough preview: every section in order, at its
planned length, showing what it will show and say. They settle the structure before any slow work is spent on it.
Pictures come next, and voice, music and polish last, on a structure they already approved.

## What defines it

The first film the person sees is complete and cheap: all of it, nothing slow. One finished section with the rest
missing is not a preview. An outline only in the chat is not one either: the preview is on the timeline, where they
can reorder, retime and rewrite it.

## What has to be true

- **Nothing slow before the preview is approved**: no voice-over, music, sound effects, generated pictures or video,
  screen recordings, renders from other programs, animated pages, and no export. It should take minutes, not tens of
  minutes: read the clock when you start and when you stop, and tell the person the real number.
- **Each section is one clip** in film.html, end to end on one track: a card with its title, what will be on screen,
  and the words planned for it (text on the card, not subtitles), lasting as long as those words take to say at a
  natural pace (about 150 words a minute in English, 250 characters a minute in Chinese). The preview is as long as
  the film is planned to be.
- **Three stops**: after the preview, after the pictures, after the sound. At each, stop and ask. What the person
  changed in Studio is the new plan: read film.html again before going on. If they say to go straight through, do.
- **Sound last**: voice-over, music and effects come once the pictures are approved, and clips are retimed to the
  real voice then. Export only when asked.
- **Counted before it is spent**: before the first `get` the person pays for, tell them what the film will ask of each
  paid service, counted (lines and characters of voice-over, minutes of music, sound effects, pictures, video clips)
  and which service makes each, and wait for a yes. OpenFilm never tells you prices; they weigh the counts against
  their own plan. A service on this computer, and OpenFilm's own library, cost nothing and need no ask.

## What you have

Studio shows every change as it lands, so the preview needs no `look`, and the cards can be plain and alike: one
design for all of them. `look` can be slow, a frame taking many seconds and a range minutes, and slower while anything
else is busy: check single frames of what you changed. A voice service running on this computer may do one job at a
time: never start a voice-over while another film's is running. Time the first slow job of each kind (a voice-over,
an animated page, a look) and tell the person how long the rest will take from that.

OpenFilm's canvas also lays a film out as a storyboard, but as notes and picture or clip nodes that are made, and paid
for, when the person presses Make; nothing is on the timeline until they send it. The preview is the timeline itself,
made for nothing. Shots laid out on the canvas belong to the pictures stage, after the preview, with their count first.

## With the person

Their words come first. Ask only what blocks the preview (the topic or material, roughly how long, landscape or
portrait); everything else can wait for their reaction to it. With the preview, tell them in a few lines the sections
and total length, read from film.html as saved, what each later stage adds, roughly how long each will take and what it will ask of paid services, so
they choose what to keep.
